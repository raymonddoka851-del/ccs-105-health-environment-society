TOPIC 8: DIGITAL ENVIRONMENT

https://www.genevaenvironmentnetwork.org/resources/updates/data-digital-technology-and-the-environment/

 A virtual place accessible through the connectedness of the Internet.

 An online space created by digital technologies, platforms and networks through

which people communicate, access information, learn, work, and interact.

Focal point

  1. Social media presence
  2. Digital footprints
  3. Use of AI
  4. Scamming & digital fraud

Core components of a digital environment

 Digital devices(hardware): Physical electronic equipment to access,

process, store and share digital information.

 Internet & connectivity (networks): Infrastructure & technologies

that enable devices to connect and exchange information.

Social media: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and LinkedIn Search engines: Google, Microsoft Bing

Learning: Google Classroom, Moodle and Microsoft Teams Communication: WhatsApp, Telegram, Zoom

Productivity: Microsoft Office (Word, Excel), Google Workspace E-commerce: Jumia, Kilimall, Amazon

 Digital platforms & applications (software): Programs & online

platforms that enable users to perform different activities;

communication, information sharing, learning, entertainment. E.g.,

Digital devices: Computers (desktops and

laptops), smartphones, tablets, smart TVs,

smartwatches, digital cameras

Internet & connectivity: Wi-Fi networks, mobile

networks (3G, 4G, 5G), fibre-optic internet connections,

Bluetooth connectivity, satellite internet service

Core components of a digital environment

 Digital content & information : Different forms of information

created, stored and shared electronically. E.g.,

Text documents, e-books, electronic reports Online newspapers, blogs, digital publications

Digital photographs, graphics, illustrations Digital maps, databases

Videos, podcasts, audio recordings Statistical datasets

 Users & digital communities : Individuals and groups who

participate in digital environments and interact through digital

platforms. E.g.,

Students attending online classes Online discussion groups and communities

Lecturers sharing learning materials through Moodle Content creators, bloggers

Professionals collaborating through LinkedIn Digital entrepreneurs

Core components of a digital environment

 Security & privacy systems: Tools and practices used to protect

devices, information, accounts and online identities. E.g.,

Artificial Intelligence (AI): ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot

Cloud computing: Google Drive, Microsoft OneDrive, Dropbox

Internet of Things (IoT): Smart home devices, connected agricultural sensors, wearable health monitors

Big data: Analysis of large datasets to identify patterns and support decision-making

Virtual & augmented reality (VR/AR): Virtual learning environments, interactive simulations.

Blockchain: Distributed digital records used in areas such as financial transactions and supply-chain tracking

Passwords & multi-factor authentication (MFA) Firewalls & encryption

Antivirus & anti-malware software Virtual Private Networks (VPNs)

Privacy settings on social media platforms Data backup & recovery systems

 Emerging technologies : Advanced technologies that are

transforming how people interact with digital systems & information.

Examples of digital environments

Digital environments are part of everyday life: we learn, work, communicate, interact, & have fun in the digital age!

  1. Social media presence

 Refers to how an individual, organization or institution is represented

and interacts with others on social media platforms.

 For university students, this involves how you create profiles, share

information, communicate and participate in online communities.

 It includes the information shared through profiles, posts, comments,

photos, videos, likes, follows, and other online interactions.

 Also includes the groups and communities joined, and the way an

individual communicates with others.

 Your social media presence contributes to your digital identity

and reputation. What you share, how you interact and how you

conduct yourself online can have lasting implications for your

personal, academic, and professional life.

Key aspects of social media presence

 Online identity: How you present yourself through your profile,

username, profile picture, biography and personal information.

 Content sharing: Information, photographs, videos, opinions, and

comments that you post or share on social media.

 Online interactions : How you communicate and engage with

others through likes, comments, messages, discussions, and

sharing of posts.

 Digital reputation: The impression others develop about you based

on your online activities, behaviour, and shared content.

 Privacy and security : Managing account privacy settings and

protecting personal information from unauthorized access or misuse.

  1. Social media presence

 Different platforms may serve different purposes. Consider the type

of information and content you share on each platform.

Snapchat Sharing photos & short videos, instant messaging, video calls

Instagram Sharing photographs, videos and stories

TikTok Creating and sharing short videos

X (Twitter) Sharing opinions, news and public discussions

Responsible social media presence

Risks associated with social media

 Cyberbullying & online harassment: Exposure to insults, threats,

intimidation, humiliation or abusive messages from other users.

 Privacy breaches & identity theft : Personal information,

photographs and account details may be exposed, stolen or

misused.

 Online scams & fraud: Fraudsters may use fake accounts,

phishing links, fraudulent scholarship offers and deceptive

promotions to steal money or personal information.

 Misinformation & disinformation:Exposure to or sharing of false,

misleading or manipulated information.

Risks associated with social media

 Reputational damage : Inappropriate posts, comments,

photographs or videos may negatively affect one's academic,

personal and future professional reputation.

 Social media addiction & reduced productivity: Excessive use

may interfere with academic work, concentration, sleep and other

daily responsibilities.

 Mental health & social pressure: Social comparison, negative

comments and pressure to gain likes and followers may contribute

to stress, anxiety and reduced self-esteem.

 Exposure to inappropriate content: Encountering harmful, violent,

sexually explicit or misleading material.

  1. Digital footprints

 Every time you go online, you leave a trail.

 There is a collection of information about you that exists on the

internet.

 A digital footprint is the unique trail of data and information you

leave behind every time you use the internet and digital platforms.

 May include social media posts, comments and reactions, photos

and videos, online searches, websites visited, online purchases,

emails and messages, and information provided when registering

for online services.

Types of digital footprints

 Two main types of digital

footprints: active and passive

Why your digital footprint matter

 Personal reputation & relationships: Online activities influence how others perceive. Online

posts and interactions can affect relationships with friends, colleagues and family.

 Academic & career opportunities : Your online presence may influence scholarship and

educational opportunities. Employers may review publicly available online information when

assessing job applicants

 Digital advertising: Online activities build personalized advertisements and recommendations.

 Privacy & security: Exposed personal information promote identity theft, fraud & privacy breaches.

 Permanence: Information online can be saved, cached, or resurface years later even after you

think it has been deleted.

  1. Scamming and digital fraud

 These are dishonest activities carried out through digital platforms

or electronic communication to deceive people into revealing

personal information, transferring money or providing access to

their accounts.

 A scam is a deceptive activity designed to trick people into giving

away money, personal information, access to accounts or other

valuable resources.

 Scammers commonly use email, SMS, phone calls, social media,

messaging applications, fake websites, and online marketplaces.

  1. Scamming and digital fraud

 Digital fraud involves using digital technologies to deliberately

deceive individuals or organizations for financial or other unlawful

gain.

 Any deceptive activity that uses the internet, computers, or mobile

devices to steal money, personal data, or sensitive information from

individuals and businesses.

 In Kenya and globally, these threats grow fast as criminal networks

use AI, social media ads, and fast mobile payment systems.

Warning signs

 Unsolicited links or attachments, weird

spelling in website URLs, or generic

greetings.

 Pressure to act immediately or send

money via non-reversible channels.

 Requests for remote access to your

phone or computer.

 Requests for passwords & PINs.

 Unverified investment or employment

opportunities.

 Offers that look "too good to be true,"

e.g., massive job pay, quick crypto

gains, or goods sold far below market

value.

Protecting yourself in the digital environment: A simple

approach is to STOP – CHECK – ACT.

 STOP: Do not immediately respond to suspicious

messages, click links or send money.

 CHECK: Verify: 1) Who sent the message? 2) Is the

request genuine? 3) Is the website or account legitimate?

  1. Is the information supported by reliable sources?

 ACT: Take appropriate action.

What to do if you are scammed?

 Contact your bank or mobile network operator

immediately to try and freeze or reverse the

transaction.

 Report the incident e.g., bank's customer support

line or dedicated reporting email.

 Change your passwords and multi-factor

authentication settings.

Key message

 As university students, it is important to develop digital competence

and digital citizenship.

 Digital competence is the ability to confidently, effectively and

critically use digital technologies to access, evaluate, create and

share information, communicate, solve problems, and adapt to

emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence

 Digital citizenship means using digital technologies responsibly,

ethically, safely, and respectfully. It involves respecting others

online, protecting personal information, observing digital rights and

responsibilities, and contributing positively to online communities
