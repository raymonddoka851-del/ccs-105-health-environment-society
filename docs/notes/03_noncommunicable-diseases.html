CCS 105: HEALTH, ENVIRONMENT , AND SOCIETY

TOPIC 3: NONCOMMUNICABLE DISEASES

 Noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) are chronic (lasting for a long

time) medical conditions that cannot be spread between individuals.

 They are the result of a combination of:

 Genetic factors

 Physiological factors

 Environmental factors

 Behavioural factors

 Among these, behavioral or lifestyle risk factors play the

dominant role.

NCDs Core risk factors

 Risk factors increase

the likelihood of

developing a disease.

 Two categories of risk

factors:

  1. Modifiable (can be

changed).

  1. Non-modifiable

(Cannot be changed).

The major groups of NCDs include:

WHO data:

 NCDs cause 74% of all

deaths worldwide.

 NCDs killed ~43 million

people in 2021.

 73% in low- & middle-

income countries.

 The four groups

account for 80% of all

premature NCD deaths.

Other notable NCDs

 Mental health disorders : Depression, anxiety disorders &

schizophrenia.

 Chronic kidney disease: Kidney function gradually declines. Closely

associated with diabetes & hypertension.

 Oral diseases: Dental caries (tooth decay) & periodontal disease

(gum disease).

 Musculoskeletal diseases: Osteoarthritis & chronic musculoskeletal

conditions, causing pain, reduced mobility, disability.

 Sickle cell disease:Inherited blood disorder that affects red blood

cells, causing anaemia, pain crises & other complications.

 Neurological diseases (affect nervous system): Epilepsy,

Parkinson's disease, Alzheimer's disease & Dementia.

A. Modifiable risk factors

 Factors that can potentially be changed through individual or public-

health interventions. They include behavioural, physiological

(metabolic), and environmental risk factors.

 Behavioural risk factors: 1) Tobacco use, including second-hand

smoke, 2) Unhealthy diets, including excess salt, sugar, and fats, 3)

Harmful use of alcohol harms the liver, 4) Physical inactivity, where

lack of regular exercise leads to weight gain and poor heart health.

 Environmental risk factors: Air pollution (indoor & outdoor)

accounts for 6.7 million deaths globally, of which about 5.6 million

are due to NCDs, including stroke, ischaemic heart disease, chronic

obstructive pulmonary disease, and lung cancer.

A. Modifiable risk factors

 Metabolic risk factors: Behavioural risk factors contribute to four

key metabolic changes that increase the risk of NCDs: 1) Raised

blood pressure (hypertension), 2) Overweight/obesity, 3) High blood

glucose levels (diabetes), 4) Abnormal blood lipids (high

cholesterol).

 Leading metabolic risk factor globally iselevated blood pressure,

followed byraised blood glucose,and overweight & obesity.

 Metabolic risk factors cannot be directly manipulated in the way that

behavioral factors can, although, with the right strategies, alteration

is possible.

Singh, A., Banerjee, P., Anas, M., Singh, N., & Qamar, I. (2020). Traditional nutritional and health practices targeting lifestyle behavioral changes in humans. Journal of Lifestyle Medicine, 10(2), 67.

B. Non-modifiable risk factors

 These are factors that cannot easily be changed. They include

biological traits and background that you cannot change or control.

 Heredity and genetics : Some individuals inherit genetic

characteristics that increase their susceptibility to some NCDs.

 A family history of conditions such as diabetes, hypertension or

certain cancers may increase an individual's risk.

 Age: The risk of many NCDs increases with age because of

cumulative exposure to risk factors and physiological changes.

 Sex/gender: Some diseases have different levels of occurrence or

risk among males and females

Diabetes mellitus: Type 2 Diabetes

 A chronic metabolic disease in whichblood glucose levels remain

abnormally highbecause the body does not produce enough insulin

or cannot use insulin effectively.

 Risk factors: 1) Overweight or obesity, 2) Physical inactivity, 3)

Unhealthy diet, 4) Family history and genetic factors, 5) Increasing

age, 5) Gestational diabetes (during pregnancy) significantly

increases the risk of developing type 2 diabetes later in life.

 Prevention and treatment: Healthy eating, regular physical activity,

maintain healthy body weight, monitor blood glucose and attend

regular health check-ups. Treatment include lifestyle changes, oral

medicines and/or insulin, depending on the type and individual needs.

 Prevent and reduce the impact of Type 2 diabetes through healthy

lifestyles and early management.

Hypertension (High blood pressure-BP)

 A chronic condition in whichblood pressure in the arteries remains

persistently elevated, increasing the risk of heart disease, stroke and

kidney damage.

 Risk factors: High-salt intake & unhealthy diet. Physical inactivity.

Overweight and obesity. Tobacco use. Harmful alcohol consumption.

Family history. Prolonged exposure to air pollution.

 Prevention and treatment: Reduce salt intake. Eat a healthy diet.

Maintain a healthy body weight. Regular physical activity. Avoid

tobacco. Limit harmful alcohol consumption. Regular BP checks. Take

prescribed medication appropriately. Limit exposure to air pollution.

 Hypertension can be silent, but regular BP checks & appropriate

treatment help prevent heart attacks, strokes & kidney damage.

Obesity

 A chronic condition characterized by excessive body fat that can

negatively affect health. It’s associated with an increased risk of type 2

diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular diseases and certain cancers.

 In adults, body mass index (BMI) over 25 is considered overweight,

and over 30 is obese.

 Risk factors: Unhealthy diet; particularly excessive intake of energy-

dense foods and high consumption of sugary foods and drinks.

Physical inactivity, Sedentary lifestyles, Genetic predisposition,

Environmental factors. Limited access to healthy foods

(socioeconomic factors).

 Obesity develops throughcomplex interactionsamong diet, physical

activity, genetics, environment, social conditions and other factors

rather than simply being a consequence of individual choices.

Obesity

 Prevention and management : Healthy balanced diets. Regular

physical activity. Reduce sedentary behaviour. Monitor body weight

and seek professional support when needed. Healthcare

professionals can provide nutrition counselling, assess health risks

and recommend appropriate treatment.

 Obesity is a complex, multifactorial health condition. Prevention and

management require healthy behaviours, access to healthcare and

supportive environments that make food choices and physical activity

more accessible.

Cancer

 A broad group of diseases characterized by uncontrolled growth &

multiplication of abnormal cells. These cells may invade surrounding

tissues & spread to other parts of the body. Cancer can develop in

almost any part of the body, and different cancers have different

causes, risk factors and treatment approaches.

 Cancer is the 2 nd leading cause of death worldwide, after

cardiovascular diseases. Most common cancers are lung, breast,

colorectal, prostate & stomach cancers.

 Risk factors: Tobacco use. Harmful alcohol consumption. Unhealthy

diets. Physical inactivity. Overweight & obesity. Exposure to cancer-

causing infections (e.g., HPV & hepatitis). Radiation & environmental

pollutants/carcinogens. Genetic predisposition & increasing age.

Many cancers can be cured if detected early & treated effectively

 Prevention and treatment: Avoid tobacco. Limit alcohol. Healthy

diets. Healthy body weight. Regular physical activity. Vaccinations

against cancer-causing infections (HPV & hepatitis B).

 Reduce exposure to ultraviolet radiation exposure. Avoid urban air

pollution & indoor smoke from household use of solid fuels.

 Participate in appropriate cancer screening. Seek medical attention for

persistent or unusual symptoms. Early diagnosis & timely treatment.

 Screening and early diagnosis promotes early detection.

 Treatment depends on the type & stage of cancer. It can include

surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or combinations of

chemotherapy, hormonal treatments & targeted biological therapies.

 Palliative care focuses on improving quality of life and relieving

symptoms for patients who have cancer (esp. advanced stages).

 Now that your understand some of the major NCDs and their

risk factors, what can you do to prevent and manage them?

 As an individual: Adopt healthy eating habits. Engage in regular

physical activity. Avoid tobacco use. Limit harmful alcohol

consumption. Maintain a healthy body weight. Participate in

recommended health screening. Follow prescribed treatment. Seek

medical attention when concerning symptoms occur.

 An important way to control NCDs is tofocus on reducing the

modifiable risk factorsassociated with these diseases.

 Invest in better management of NCDs: Regular screening. Early

detection. Treating these diseases. Access to palliative care for

people in need.

Reducing the burden of NCDs is a shared responsibility of

individuals, health professionals & communities.

 Start treatment on time

 Prevent complications
