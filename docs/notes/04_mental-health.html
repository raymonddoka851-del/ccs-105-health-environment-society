04 · Mental Health

Understand the concept

Mental health is part of health and wellbeing: it affects how people think, feel, relate to others, cope with stress, learn, and make decisions. It exists on a continuum and can change over time. A mental health condition is not a character flaw, and distress should not be diagnosed from a brief description or stereotype.

Influences and support

Mental health is shaped by interacting factors: biology and physical health; life events and coping resources; relationships and community; safety, discrimination, housing, education, and income; and access to appropriate services. Protective supports can include trusted relationships, sleep and activity routines, coping skills, belonging, and timely professional care.

Helpful response

  • Listen respectfully, ask what support would help, and avoid judgment or pressure.
  • Encourage contact with a qualified health professional or locally available support service when distress persists or disrupts daily life.
  • In an immediate safety crisis, seek urgent local emergency or clinical help and involve a trusted person if safe to do so.
  • Protect privacy, except where disclosure is necessary to address an immediate risk under applicable rules.

Common misconceptions

  • Mental health is not only the absence of mental illness.
  • People can experience mental illness and still study, work, and contribute.
  • “Just be positive” is not a treatment plan. Support should be respectful, evidence-informed, and suited to the person.

Active recall

  1. Explain the mental-health continuum in your own words.
  2. Name three different levels of influence on mental wellbeing.
  3. How could a student respond supportively to a friend who says they are struggling?
