# 01 · Holistic Wellness

## Core idea

**Holistic wellness** views health as the interaction of a whole person with their relationships, community, work, culture, and environment. It is broader than simply having no illness. The dimensions overlap: poor sleep, for example, can affect physical health, mood, concentration, and relationships.

## Dimensions to know

| Dimension | What it concerns | A practical example |
|---|---|---|
| Physical | Movement, nutrition, rest, prevention, and appropriate care | Regular activity, balanced meals, and seeking care when needed |
| Emotional / psychological | Recognizing feelings, coping, self-esteem, and purpose | Naming stress and choosing a safe coping strategy |
| Social | Supportive relationships and participation in community | Maintaining friendships and asking for support |
| Intellectual | Curiosity, learning, critical thinking, and applying knowledge | Learning a skill and evaluating evidence |
| Spiritual | Meaning, values, purpose, and connection | Reflecting on values; spirituality need not be religious |
| Occupational | Meaning, satisfaction, skills, and balance in work or study | Setting boundaries between study and rest |
| Environmental | Safe, supportive surroundings and care for shared resources | Clean water, safe housing, and community clean-up |
| Cultural | Connection to identity, traditions, and respectful belonging | Maintaining meaningful cultural ties without excluding others |
| Economic | Financial security, resources, and economic conditions | Budgeting while recognizing structural barriers to income |
| Climate-related | Effects of climate and extreme weather on wellbeing | Heat, floods, or drought affecting health and livelihoods |

These are useful lenses, not isolated boxes. A person’s wellbeing is also shaped by social determinants such as education, housing, income, access to health services, and safety.

## Apply the idea

If a student is exhausted and falling behind, a holistic response asks about more than motivation: sleep, workload, health, finances, relationships, the learning environment, and available support may all matter. Choose a response that addresses the relevant causes without blaming the person.

## Active recall

1. Explain why wellness is broader than absence of disease.
2. Name four dimensions and give a distinct example for each.
3. Give one example of how two dimensions can influence each other.

**Self-check:** A strong answer defines the dimension and explains the example. Avoid treating the dimensions as independent or suggesting that every person has identical needs.
