# 10 · Unemployment and Poverty Mitigation in Kenya

## Definitions and types

**Unemployment** generally refers to people who are available for and seeking work but do not have employment; statistical definitions specify how availability and job search are measured. It differs from **underemployment**, where people’s work or hours do not fully use their capacity or needs.

| Type | Main mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Frictional | Time spent moving between jobs or entering the labour market | A graduate searching for a first suitable role |
| Structural | Skills or location mismatch as the economy changes | Workers needing retraining after a sector changes |
| Cyclical | Lower demand during an economic downturn | Firms reduce staffing when sales fall |
| Seasonal | Work is available only in particular seasons | Some agricultural or tourism jobs |

## Poverty and its drivers

Poverty involves deprivation of resources and opportunities, not only low income. Its causes can include limited decent work, unequal access to education and services, health shocks, discrimination, insecurity, economic downturns, and environmental shocks. These causes can reinforce one another.

## Responses

Strategies discussed in the course include quality education and skills development, career guidance, entrepreneurship support, access to finance and markets, infrastructure, fair labour conditions, social protection, and policies that support job creation. Evaluate a strategy by asking who benefits, whether decent work is created, whether access is fair, and whether outcomes can be sustained.
