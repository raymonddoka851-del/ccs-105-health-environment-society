Any process that causes adjustments to a climate system could be described as creating the phenomenon by which certain gases, so-called greenhouse gases in the atmosphere trap heat that would otherwise escape to space, thereby keeping the planet warm. and they have always caused warming. If there were no greenhouse effect, the planet would be badly cold more than 30 °C colder than the hospitable current average of 150C.

2 Greenhouse Gases: Greenhouse gases are gases that can trap heat. They get their name from greenhouses. A greenhouse is full of windows that let in sunlight. That sunlight creates warmth. The big trick at warmth escape. Carbon Dioxide (CO2): Made up of carbon and oxygen, it comes from decaying and living organisms and several other sources. CO2 is released when burning fossil fuels, coal and oil. -induced global warming. Methane (CH4): Methane, made of carbon and hydrogen, is a normal gas released from wetlands, growing rice, raising cattle, using natural gas and mining coal. It traps a lot of heat in the atmosphere, scientists consider it the second most important contributor to human-induced global warming of all the greenhouse gases. Ozone (O3): Up in the atmosphere where the aircraft radiation, which protects us from the powerful radiation from the sun. Close to the ground ozone acts as a greenhouse gas and can be formed by burning gas in cars and factories. Nitrous Oxide (N2O): Nitrous Oxide is a natural part of the nitrogen cycle. Nitrous Oxide released by some of the factories, power plants and fertilizers. This N2O damages the protective ozone layer. ChloroFluoroCarbons (CFCs): CFCs have no natural source but were entirely synthesized for such diverse uses as refrigerants, aerosol propellants and cleaning solvents. Their creation was in 1928 and since then concentrations of CFCs in the atmosphere have been rising. So what would happen? 1. 5 Strategies to Mitigate Climate Change 1. Carbon sequestration Carbon sequestration, also known as carbon capture and storage (CCS) entails technologies aimed at capturing CO2 emissions from power plants, industrial facilities and storing it underground for an indefinite period of time. This prolonged storage is aimed at delaying global warming. 2. Carbon sink It is a human-made reservoir that gather and retain carbon-containing compounds from the atmosphere, thereby reducing the amount of CO2 in the atmosphere. The two global carbon sinks are the ocean and the planted vegetation. These two have been emphasized since the signing of the Kyoto Protocol. 3. Carbon Credit This is a permit that authorizes the holder to release a SPECIFIC amount of CO2 and other greenhouse gases. Each credit permits the emission of 1 metric ton of CO2 or its equivalent in other gases. Entities with emission allowances are allowed to emit up to a defined limit, which is progressively reduced over time. Subsequently, companies are allowed to sell any unused credit to other companies that may need it. If one exceeds their credit, they have to subscribe to more, and they can get incentives by selling carbon credits that they have in surplus. 4. Carbon Tax This is a levy imposed on companies that produce, distribute, and utilize fossil fuels, determined by the quantity of carbon generated during combustion. This promotes research into renewable energy source, leaving the fossil fuels. 5. Geo-engineering These are the techniques employed to help in cooling the atmosphere such as: a.
