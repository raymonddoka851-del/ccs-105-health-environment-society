# 07 · Climate Change: Adaptation and Mitigation

## Two essential responses

**Climate change** is a long-term change in climate patterns, including changes driven by human greenhouse-gas emissions. **Mitigation** reduces emissions or increases carbon storage to limit future warming. **Adaptation** reduces vulnerability and prepares people and systems for actual or expected climate effects. The two approaches complement one another.

| Challenge | Adaptation example | Mitigation example |
|---|---|---|
| Drought and unreliable rainfall | Drought planning, water conservation, resilient crops | Efficient energy and lower-emission land use |
| Flooding | Early warning, drainage, safer land-use planning | Reduce greenhouse-gas emissions |
| Heat | Shade, cooling plans, occupational precautions | Energy efficiency and cleaner power |

## Equity and resilience

Climate impacts are not shared equally. Exposure and ability to recover depend on income, housing, health, location, livelihoods, infrastructure, and public services. Effective plans use local knowledge, reliable information, inclusive decision-making, and attention to people most at risk.

### Exam-ready comparison

Ask: **Does this action address the cause of climate change (mitigation), or reduce harm from its effects (adaptation)?** Some actions can do both, but explain the pathway rather than simply naming the activity.
