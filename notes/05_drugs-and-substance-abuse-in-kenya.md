# 05 · Drugs and Substance Use in Kenya

## Key terms

**Psychoactive substances** affect mood, perception, behaviour, or cognition. Substance use ranges from use without a diagnosed disorder to harmful use and dependence; terms should be used carefully and without stigma. **Substance-use disorder** is a health condition assessed by qualified professionals, not a label to apply to someone from a single behaviour.

## Why use and harm occur

Possible influences include availability, peer and family environments, stress or trauma, mental and physical health, economic conditions, social norms, and individual factors. No single cause explains every person’s experience. Alcohol, tobacco/nicotine, and other drugs have different effects; risks depend on substance, amount, frequency, route, and the person’s health.

## Consequences and support

Potential harms include injury, dependence, overdose, effects on physical or mental health, disrupted study or work, family stress, financial hardship, and legal consequences. Do not assume every person experiences all harms. A compassionate, practical response prioritizes safety and access to care.

### Prevention and recovery

- Prevention combines accurate education, supportive families and communities, life skills, reduced harmful availability, and accessible services.
- Treatment may involve clinical assessment, counselling, rehabilitation, medication where appropriate, and peer or family support.
- Recovery is individual and may include setbacks; avoid shame and encourage qualified care.
- Suspected overdose or immediate danger requires urgent local emergency medical assistance.

## Kenya: law and policy

The course source discusses Kenyan laws and policies that regulate alcohol and control narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances, including the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (Control) Act. Laws can change and details depend on the conduct and current legislation; consult current official Kenyan sources rather than memorizing an outdated penalty or treating substance use as synonymous with crime.
