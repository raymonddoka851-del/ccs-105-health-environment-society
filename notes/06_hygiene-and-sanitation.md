# 06 · Hygiene and Sanitation

## The public-health purpose

**Hygiene** means practices that help maintain health and reduce exposure to disease. **Sanitation** concerns safe systems and conditions for managing human waste and maintaining a clean environment. Safe water, hand hygiene, food safety, toilets, drainage, and waste management work together.

## Practical prevention chain

1. **Water:** protect sources, treat or boil water when advised, and store it in clean covered containers.
2. **Hands:** wash with soap and safe water at key times, including after toilet use and before preparing or eating food.
3. **Food:** keep preparation areas clean, separate raw and cooked foods, cook thoroughly, and store safely.
4. **Waste and toilets:** use and maintain safe sanitation; dispose of waste appropriately and prevent contamination of water sources.
5. **Community environment:** control stagnant water and pests, maintain drainage, and use safe cleaning methods.

## Why infrastructure matters

Hygiene is not only an individual responsibility. Reliable water, accessible toilets, waste collection, safe housing, and public education shape whether healthy practices are possible. Effective sanitation protects households and the wider community.
